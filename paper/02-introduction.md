# Introduction
Public transaction records help housing buyers identify comparable sales and examine how observed prices vary across locations and property types. A sale with a separately priced parking space and a sale with a bundled parking price can look comparable even though their price and area fields describe different transfer bundles. Taiwan's Ministry of the Interior (MOI) documents these reporting conventions and explains that the public service excludes some exceptional transactions [@moi]. A useful comparison therefore needs a declared sample definition and price convention alongside the reported numbers.

Consider a request to compare residential towers in two districts over the same period. The system must resolve the districts, select a common price definition, retrieve comparable cohorts, and explain exclusions. It may also need to check which version of a data definition applied at a particular date. An answer can appear plausible while using different parking adjustments in each district or comparing cohorts with unequal time coverage. Retrieving the relevant documents and producing the numerical summary are separate operations, and both require evidence.

We propose a bounded agent for descriptive housing comparison. Taichung is the candidate study area. The final definition of eligible existing homes depends on source coverage and confirmed field semantics. The study asks two questions. RQ1 asks whether adaptive tool selection improves supported task completion on conditional and multistep housing queries when a fixed workflow has access to the same tools and evidence. RQ2 asks how the workflows differ in appropriate abstention, partially supported answers, and resource use when evidence is incomplete.

The studies in Section~\ref{sec:related} motivate price analysis, retrieval, and agent control. The comparison proposed here focuses on how these controllers handle the same evidence under the same conditions for a housing task.

The proposed design includes an evidence contract for numerical and documentary claims, a response policy for missing or conflicting evidence, and a paired evaluation protocol. Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews housing analytics and tool-using agents. Section~\ref{sec:method} describes the architecture. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} presents the snapshot audit and evaluation protocol. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} summarizes the findings and limits.
